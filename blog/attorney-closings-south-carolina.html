Why South Carolina Closings Are Handled by Attorneys

By Morgan Hartwell · September 2026

If you are moving to South Carolina from another state, closing day may look different from what you expect. Here, the closing is handled by an attorney, not a title or escrow company working on its own.

Why an Attorney?

South Carolina treats real estate closings as the practice of law. The South Carolina Bar's Ethics Advisory Opinion 05-18 explains that each phase of a residential closing must be supervised by a licensed South Carolina attorney. That covers work like the title search and the preparation of closing documents.

What the Closing Attorney Does

The attorney's office reviews the title and prepares the deed and settlement documents. It also makes sure the money moves where it should. They also record the deed with the county once everything is signed.

Buyers often choose the closing attorney, especially when a lender is involved. We are happy to share a few names so you can compare.

What to Expect on Closing Day

Plan on bringing a government-issued photo ID and confirming your wire or certified funds with the attorney's office by phone before you send anything. Wire fraud is real, so never follow wiring instructions that arrive by email alone.

This post is general information, not legal or tax advice. Rules and deadlines can change, so confirm the details with your attorney or county office.
