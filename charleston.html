About Charleston

Getting to Know Charleston

Charleston is the largest city in South Carolina by population, and most people picture the same few blocks when they hear its name. In practice the city covers a lot of ground, from the historic peninsula to suburban neighborhoods west of the Ashley River and on the sea islands.

That range matters when you buy. A home on the peninsula comes with historic review, while a home in West Ashley or on James Island usually has newer construction and a yard. We help you compare them side by side before you spend a weekend touring.

Real Estate

Homes in Charleston

The Charleston Old and Historic District was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1960. Exterior changes there go through the city's Board of Architectural Review, which dates back to the city's 1931 zoning ordinance, so plan for that if you want to renovate.

Outside the peninsula you will find a wider mix of ranch homes, newer subdivisions, townhomes and condos. Daniel Island is a master-planned community with its own zoning plan.

Check the Flood Zone

Charleston County's flood information explains FEMA zones. Zones A and V are Special Flood Hazard Areas, where flood insurance is usually required for a mortgage. We pull the flood zone for every home you consider before you write an offer.

Questions

Charleston FAQs

Is Daniel Island part of Charleston?

Yes. Daniel Island is within the City of Charleston and has its own master-planned zoning. Confirm the county and school district for a specific address before you buy.

Do historic homes have renovation rules?

Inside the historic district, exterior changes go through the Board of Architectural Review. Interior work usually does not. We share what we know about a home's history before you buy.

Which school district serves Charleston?

Most of the city is served by Charleston County School District. Always confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the district.

Can you show me homes on the peninsula and in the suburbs?

Yes. Most of our Charleston buyers compare at least two parts of the city before they decide, and we build tours around that.
